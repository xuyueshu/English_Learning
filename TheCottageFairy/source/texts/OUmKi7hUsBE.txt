Winter has finally come to an end. This is a time of renewed energy and hope. And in my corner of the world, the weather is perfect for the annual balloon festival. This is one of my favorite events of the year. There's something about these amazing flying machines in the sky that gives me a real childlike delight. It feels like I'm momentarily falling into some kind of dream. Luke and I have a tradition of going together every year and seeing them off . Watching them, we realized that in a year we couldn't wait to share this experience with our little one. In this video, I

wanted to share an observation I made many years ago. I received an alternative education , and one of the defining features of my childhood was that I spent most of my time offline. Partly because of my age and the fact that cassettes were still popular when I was born, but also because I honestly didn't have much interest in it. It wasn't until I entered university that I started using something other than a flip phone and created my first social media account, mostly to overcome my severe anxiety and find resources that would help me learn how to cope with it. I sought out online communities of people who were also trying to recover from these debilitating conditions. I was shocked by the number of people who told me that what

I was feeling was forever. Like, it's just a part of me. I just had to learn to live with it. There was no point in trying to be better, because if I could heal and fully recover, it would mean that things weren't so bad after all. For some reason, these words stuck with me, and I deeply learned them for many years. I sincerely believed that there was nothing I could do to improve my inner state. However, I kept moving forward, and soon, as I grew older, I met many wonderful people who helped me on my journey, as well as many free online therapists who shared their knowledge on YouTube. I practiced what I was taught. I persistently put this into practice and very slowly began to feel better. I

learned about neuroplasticity, started regulating my emotions, and made progress I never dreamed of. I'm not saying it's an easy path, and everyone's situation is different. There is no universal solution. I just wanted to say that I think it can be dangerous to be so negative about yourself and others, without considering other points of view. It has been well proven by scientists that negatives sell better. It gets clicks, it gets engagement, it gets exciting, and it feels right, even though it has also been proven to lead to increased anxiety and depression. And I don't say this to devalue fear or all the terrible things happening in the world at any given moment. I'm

simply talking about how we allow this knowledge to influence how we treat others and ourselves. In my short life, the

only thing I truly know is that our nature, like the seasons , the moon, life itself—is to change and grow. I spent so many years identifying with the labels I was given, to the point where I believed that was who I was. Now I describe myself as someone who experiences anxiety like everyone else , but that's not who I am. This is not a permanent trait of my personality. This is one of the reasons I love making these videos, because if there's one message I want to repeat, it's that you are capable, you are strong, and your soul craves growth and healing. Not everyone will believe in you. Some will try to hinder you. There will always be a lot of fear and bitterness in the world at any given point in

history . However, you can be a source of light in the darkness. And I will tell you a secret. Once you decide to become that source of starlight, you will realize that there are entire galaxies of stars and that you are far from alone. Gradually recovering from these natural human challenges, I began to let more new things into my life . New relationships, scary things, wonderful things, stressful things—all of which are part of a fulfilling life. I still sometimes take

steps back, and each moment teaches me the power of believing in myself: I am not helpless and I am not static. I can change, and things can get better. And for me, there is no better symbol of this than spring, when everything is renewed and what seemed dead and gone begins to grow again. A few weeks ago, I

released a video about big changes in your career and future, and how important it is to stay true to your personal values. What inspires me is that each year I am more consistently developing a positive inner life and inner dialogue, and it is a slow, difficult, but extremely rewarding journey. Every day I feel more and more calm and content, and it's such an incredible feeling. I can't even imagine where I will be in the future. I look forward to growing older, making new discoveries, and continuing this exciting adventure of learning. But I also realized that an

absolutely essential part of this journey is optimism. And to clarify: this does not mean avoiding negative things. This does not mean not feeling criticism, pessimism, fear, or despair at times, which I know are very common at any point in history. For me, being an optimist is about balancing those emotions, what you consume, and everything you let into your inner world to understand whether it helps or hinders your path, and what it says about your internal dialogue and attitude towards yourself and others. I find it very interesting that on the Internet, when I talked the most

about being satisfied or very happy, I often received more criticism. And I find that really interesting. I was reading a book about making career decisions and defining success through internal state, not external standards. One interesting chapter discussed optimists who believe in themselves, in their ability to be happy and overcome difficulties; about people who find meaning in difficult circumstances, are resilient in the face of suffering, believe in the goodness of others, have hope for a better world and future, believe in humanity's ability to build it, proactively support these things, and treat

people with kindness and compassion. People who have a very meaningful and joyful inner life. It was fascinating to read these studies, decades of studies that showed that people who are able to find the positive in life's circumstances live longer, have better health, and fewer stress-related illnesses. These studies were striking because I definitely didn't consider myself a positive person for much of my youth, and only now do I feel like I'm becoming someone

who feels inner peace. I think it's very easy to simplify the concept of positivity and put a label on someone who is always happy. But I don't think that's what positivity is at all. I think it's about acknowledging all of our emotions and choosing to nurture those feelings, that internal dialogue, and those people in our lives who help us, not hinder us. Anyway, I decided to just share this. It was something very simple, but since I read it in that book, it really struck me, you know, as something I want to make part of my life. I want to develop these things because I know it brings me joy, and maybe

it's something that will resonate with you, and maybe it won't. I don't know, but I just wanted to share this because it was extremely important for overcoming very strong anxiety at one point when it seemed like my inner world was a scary place, and changing that was so helpful, although not easy, but very helpful.
