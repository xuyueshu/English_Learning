And this can come in handy on your creative path, or in the process of rethinking your career, or, say, when trying to master a hobby that you want to take to a farmers market or craft fair with , like I once did. A friend of mine advised me, even before

I started thinking about starting a business or turning a hobby into a side job or anything like that, that no matter what I was taking on—whether it was a project or just a decision in my then-career as an editor and bookseller—it was a good idea to think carefully about your personal philosophy about what you were doing before you started anything. I think this is especially useful when you're trying to start your own business, create a YouTube channel, or anything else. What message are you trying to convey? And how can you best convey this philosophy? When I first thought about making my

first video, I took the time to carefully consider what message my channel should convey, and I realized: I want it to convey what I'm really missing in my life, what I need to remind myself of when things get too much. Having that philosophy helped me a lot when the channel suddenly started growing almost overnight, which was overwhelming, but luckily I already knew what I wanted, so it wasn't as scary as it might have been if I had just put up random videos without thinking about their content. I see my videos as artistic short

films that provide refuge from the noise and bustle to those who need it. So in my case, since the videos are supposed to be meditative, I decided not to insert ads in the middle of the videos so as not to disrupt the peaceful atmosphere I was trying to create. I did the same with sponsorship. I didn't want to do any sponsorship integrations because that would again look like destroying the sense of peace. Of course, I should add that advertising or sponsorship in videos is a great thing, and I 100% support creators who do so, especially since many of them have to rely on it to make a

living. Of course, having a clear philosophy does not guarantee the success of your business. I've been very lucky in this regard, I feel very grateful that my videos have been noticed and my business has developed, but unfortunately, that's not always the case. And why I still think having a philosophy is important is because even if it were just my weekend job— spending a few hours each week packing a few orders or working on a little video, even if it remained a hobby for years, I would still know that it was something that was very close to my heart.
