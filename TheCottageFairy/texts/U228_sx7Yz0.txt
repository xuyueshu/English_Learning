Summer at the cottage (and mushroom hunting)
2021-06-10

so in many places around the world rainfall is a given it comes and goes without being much of an event but here in my home we celebrate the rainfall knowing we will soon go months with barely any the land will become parched

and dry and prone to wildfires so so so

it is shortly after the rainfall that a very elusive but delectable life form appears on the forest floor they are known as morels and often grow in disturbed areas or ones that have experienced wildfires they're absolutely delicious and very different from the mushrooms you usually see in the stores

i mentioned this once before one of my greatest fears is fire i've experienced a home fire and it is terrifying when i shared this with a friend they wondered why i would move to a place that regularly experiences wildfires i suppose my answer is because sometimes the benefits of a decision far outweigh the risk i am so happy here and no matter what happens i wouldn't want to be anywhere else when i was a teenager i was living in

cuba during hurricane sandy when i lived on the coastline we feared tsunamis and earthquakes wherever we went there was risk and here it is no different as tourist season comes around i become a little more anxious knowing that many non-locals either unaware or not concerned with safety and will light campfires even though they are prohibited the smoke jumpers are also vigilant and my town prepares accordingly we water the grass around our homes and put together a box of valuables in my box i put all my documents and pet supplies and some of my favorite paintings and books i try to be practical and only take what

is needed or most important to me at any point we could lose everything the memory of fires is everywhere in my valley where miles of dead trees stretch across hillsides but to dwell on fear and worry won't resolve the problem instead i let those emotions take hold of my heart for a few minutes hold them there and respect that this is something that is on my mind then i try to force myself to let it go and instead turn to asking myself what can i do the answer is usually to donate to my local nature conservancy and as a teacher we would invite smoke jumpers to visit our classrooms and speak to our children it is hard for me to remember sometimes but there are so many proactive things to do

instead of worry in our world right now it is easy to see things through the eyes of negativity and cynicism but i will always believe that that feeds despair instead of cultivating hope and action and unity even as i accept the possibility that the fires may take away what i love i know that i cannot live fearing what may or may not happen instead i look to nature and see that while i waste time worrying that it may be destroyed it is thriving and abundant and alive with wildfires come new plants and undergrowth that feed the animals a variety of plants that thrive in ashy soil and of course delicious mushrooms in the midst of destruction i try to see the hope

being rebuilt and of course enjoying the delicacies that nature has to offer i hope you have a lovely day or night so so

do you
