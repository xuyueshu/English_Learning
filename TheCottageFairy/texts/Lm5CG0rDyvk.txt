on living a ‘boring’ life - accepting a gentle daily existence
2023-10-24

The end of October is near, and I notice all the small creatures in the forest bustling around, preparing for the coming cold weather. Birds and bears are eating their fill, and little squirrels are stocking up. I'm also preparing for change and looking forward to seeing how my world will transform when the first hard frosts hit. Sometimes I would call life in my rural valley pretty boring by society's standards. But in reality, boredom is a relative concept , and I think that our worldview and perception of the world largely determine how interested we are in experiencing the wonders of this life, as well as being ready for the challenges it can bring. Living here, I don't go out often

. Sometimes the most exciting event is a trip to the grocery store. During the week, besides a lot of work, I often go for walks in nature with a good friend, spend time with my pets, and admire the stars on clear nights. It's a calm, rhythmic existence that forces you to come face to face with yourself, especially when the cold weather sets in and you wake up to a quiet, snowy world, waiting for the snowplow. When I was young, I felt bored quite often. Sometimes I would go shopping and feel a brief excitement from buying new clothes or other things, which helped to calm my discomfort with myself. During certain

periods of my reclusive life, I tried to surround myself with people to drown out my own thoughts and feelings. I didn't like being alone, and if it lasted long, I desperately needed some external stimulus. I think that when we

become restless or lose interest in the everyday, when we yearn for something more—it's an opportunity to understand why we really feel that way. My walks in the forest have shown me that there are many living beings who are simply happy by the very fact of their existence. They don't need to be entertained. And I realized that whenever I felt bored, it was often a deeper challenge—to explore something previously unknown, whether within myself or outside myself. I believe that a simple,

ordinary life, lived quietly and peacefully, is not boring at all. If we see each day as a chance to become a better version of ourselves, to think, exist, and find something meaningful, then every minute counts. As we grow older, we see that the myth of what makes life exciting or extraordinary is quite superficial. We realize that every person, regardless of whether they live in a bustling city, a remote forest, or somewhere

in between, is capable of living consciously and meaningfully. And if lived with dignity, every life is as unique and extraordinary as any other. Of course, I try to do quite practical things to keep my life dynamic. I journal a lot and love setting goals, especially in reframing my thinking to be kinder to myself.

I also like to take field guides with me into the woods to discover new plants and creatures. I seek out my community and reconnect with people who are kind to me and encourage me to grow. And I try to give love as often as possible, in any way, even just by smiling, helping, or being there for those who are dear to me. All of this helps me see the beauty of life, and every day becomes a gift. Of course, when life flies by quickly, it's hard for me to enjoy the moment. So I try to slow down and remember that planning is important, but being is even more important. No one can live life without experiencing pain or discomfort. We all carry within us memories

and experiences, both good and bad. Each of us has questions about our own existence and the potential to replace fear with wonder at the mystery of life. Gradually letting go of what holds us back and finding joy in pursuing that greater thing that fills us. Something that cannot be bought, but can only be nurtured. If I feel anxious or everything around me seems too mundane, I don't run to the store and desperately look for some distraction. I like to sit and ask myself: why is this so and what can I do about it? There is usually a hidden layer in my soul that desperately needs to be revealed. And these small acts of self-love , the ability to listen instead of avoiding who we are, leads to something powerful. So yes, I see my life as

far from boring. But I also know that the wonder and delight I feel is not always visible to others in a tangible way. It's more like what I cherish in my heart, and that's enough for me. Maybe this will be enough for you too. Because your life is already special and full of possibilities. It doesn't depend on where you live, how much you travel, or what you own. Living in the forest at this time of year is very pleasant: it is interesting to watch how animals prepare for winter, store food

and hide cones. This is very exciting and incredibly interesting. By the way, we discovered that a small wood rat had taken up residence in our shed. Although he began to build an extraordinary nest there out of all sorts of interesting things. They are also called merchant rats because they like to collect shiny objects, such as jewelry or bottle caps. These are quite interesting creatures, and they also have very fluffy tails. They are nothing like the regular rats with bare tails that you are used to. They are the indigenous inhabitants of this area. They didn't come from Europe, and they are so cute. Unfortunately, it started

gnawing holes in the shed and almost reached the electrical wires, which would have been dangerous for both the rat and us. So we decided that for the safety of both parties, it would be better to relocate this little rat. We looked for the best place to move him and found a great spot, far enough away from houses, where we released him. They are a very important species for monitoring the state of the ecosystem as a whole in this area. So it was a great opportunity to get closer to nature in October.
